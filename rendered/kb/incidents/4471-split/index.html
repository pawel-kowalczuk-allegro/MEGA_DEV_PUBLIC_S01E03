Parser: month-first interpretation for day-first tenants

The shared parser treats a day-first tenant’s date as month first. For example, R-41203 reports a booking for 2 December whose invite says 12 February. R-41200 and its correction R-41201 illustrate why the wording alone is not reliable.

The scope includes app and email reports as well as booking-v2 form reports; app and email can still reach this cause for tenants that run booking-v3. Fix date interpretation at the parser boundary, using the tenant’s date format rather than the reporter’s browser locale. Test ambiguous dates, dates whose day exceeds twelve, and month-first tenants as a control.

The cause table preserves the desk’s one-day-move caveat. Those reports describe a different symptom but the supplied classifier files them under parser. Their text alone does not prove a separate timezone bug; keep the discrepancy visible and investigate before splitting again.

Hint: booking-v3 instructs month first but reads day first

For day-first tenants on booking-v3, the form prints MM/DD/YYYY, but reads the value in the tenant’s day-first order. This is a separate form-hint defect, not fixed merely by changing the shared parser. Affected tenant handles are alder.physio, copper.studio, and pale.harbour. Align the displayed hint and the form’s interpretation with the tenant locale, and test the date that reaches the booking and invite.

The corresponding tickets are parser issue 1 and form-hint issue 2. Each includes the computed unique report count and report IDs for audit. The table above shows the original desk export’s ticket ownership, before these new tickets were filed.
